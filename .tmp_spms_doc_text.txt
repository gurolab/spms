CHAPTER I
Introduction
Background of the Study
In today's fast-paced environment, efficient supply office management is important. Computerized systems offer significant advantages by automating transactions, improving data organization, and enhancing overall operational efficiency, leading to better resource allocation and cost savings. According to (Laudon & Laudon, 2020) computerized systems play a vital role in the modern technological world by enhancing organization, increasing efficiency, and improving the way transactions are conducted in businesses and educational institutions alike.
Within the current acceleration, efficient resource management is critical to the success and sustainability of any organization. One of the key areas requiring effective oversight is the supply office, where procurement, inventory tracking, and asset distribution are routinely handled. Traditional manual systems often result in inefficiencies such as delayed transactions, data inaccuracies, and poor inventory visibility, which can hamper operational performance.
Cotabato State University's Supply and Property Office, under the direction of Engr. John A. Allesa, MIS, and his four-person staff, is responsible for the efficient management of supplies and equipment across the university's thirty two (32) offices and nine (9) departments. This includes acquisition, maintenance, and disposal. Currently, the supply office uses MS Excel for record-keeping, resulting in manual monitoring, recording, and data retrieval. This process is inefficient, especially when preparing inventory and consolidated reports. Furthermore, the manual processing of supply requests using physical documents increases the risk of misfiling, leading to delays in distribution and difficulties in retrieving information. The total volume of facilities and equipment distributed across various offices makes it difficult to accurately track and maintain up-to-date records.
To address these challenges, this capstone project proposes the development of a Supply and Property Management System—a computerized solution designed to efficient supply-related processes within an organization. The system aims to automate key functions such as requisition management, stock monitoring, asset tracking, and reporting, thereby reducing human error and improving overall efficiency.
By integrating modern software tools and database management systems, the Supply and Property Management System will provide a platform for managing supplies, improving transaction accuracy, and ensuring timely delivery of resources. This project not only highlights the practical application of information technology in administrative functions but also supports better decision-making and resource allocation within the institution.
Statement of the Problem
The Supply and Property Office is facing challenges in difficulty in handling university’s office transactions, which includes the following problems:
The current manual system for recording, monitoring, and retrieving documents related to office supplies, facilities, and equipment at university is inefficient and time-consuming. This leads to delays in processing supply requests, inaccurate inventory records, and wasted staff time, ultimately delaying the university's operational efficiency. 
Inaccurate inventory records and inefficient request processing for office supplies and equipment result in frequent stockouts, wasted resources due to overstocking, and delays in providing essential supplies to faculty and staff. 
The current manual processes for generating supply reports at university are time-consuming and cumbersome, placing a significant burden on staff and diverting their time from other essential tasks

Scope and Limitation of the Project
Simon & Goes (2013) defines scope of the study as the parameters under which the study will be operating. Scope of the study therefore explains the nature, coverage, setting and how it will be operated in relation to extent and timeframe of the study.
 Giver the Definition mentioned above, this capstone project will design and develop an web-based Supply and Property Management System to improve the efficiency and accountability of supply management at the university. The system will centralize record-keeping of office supplies and equipment and automate the processes of managing, verifying, and distributing requests to all offices.
The system also offers real-time inventory management, continuously monitoring the availability of office supplies and equipment. Automated reporting features ensure the timely generation of accurate reports. The system encompasses the following core functionalities:
Recording
The system will record and store all data related to office supplies and equipment within the university, including details such as item descriptions, quantities, acquisition dates, current status, and storage locations.
Filtering
The system will provide advanced filtering and search options to enable users to quickly locate specific office supplies and equipment based on various criteria such as item name, category, availability, and location.
On-time Request
The system will focus on facilitating the timely submission and processing of supply requests from various departments or offices.
Inventory
The system will support real-time tracking and updating of office supplies and equipment inventory across multiple office locations. It will allow authorized users to monitor stock levels, add new items, update existing records, and generate inventory reports to ensure availability and proper resource allocation.
Generation of Reports
The system will enable timely and automated generation of essential supply office reports, including inventory status, supply requisition history, stock issuance logs, and monthly consumption summaries
Printing of Documents
The system will include a document generation and printing feature to allow authorized users to produce and print key documents such as supply requisition forms, inventory summaries, delivery receipts, and asset reports.

Operational Definition of Terms
To ensure better understanding and clarity within the context of this research study, the following terms are defined operationally.
Record: Refers to the stored data within the system that pertains to office supplies and equipment, including details such as item names, quantities, dates of transactions, and responsible personnel.
Intranet. A private network accessible only within the institution or office, used to securely share information and system resources among authorized users.
Filter. A system feature that allows users to narrow down and retrieve specific items or data based on defined criteria, improving the speed and accuracy of searches.
Reports – Refers to the system-generated documents or summaries that provide real-time data on inventory levels, supply usage, and transaction history to support monitoring and decision-making.
Data Migration - Data migration refers to the systematic process of transferring existing inventory data, purchase order records, and supplier information from the current manual system to the new Supply and Property Management System database.
Supply Office Transactions - Supply Office Transaction: Any action related to the management of university assets, including the recording of asset acquisition, assignment to departments, tracking of maintenance, and disposal of assets.
Access - The ability of authorized users to generate and view reports from the computerized system, based on their assigned role and permissions. This includes access to various types of reports, such as inventory reports, purchase order reports, and budget reports.
Inventory - Inventory refers to all supplies and equipment managed by the Supply and Property Management System, including office supplies, technology equipment, and furniture.
Tracking - The process of recording, processing, and monitoring the status of each supply request submitted through the Supply and Property Management System. Successful tracking is defined as the timely and accurate update of request status, with no discrepancies between the system record and the actual status of the request
Transparency - refers to the system's ability to provide all stakeholders (including staff, faculty, and administrators) with readily accessible and understandable information regarding supply requests, inventory levels, purchase orders, and expenditures. This includes real-time access to data through user-friendly interfaces and clear, concise reporting tools.
Dashboard - the main user interface screen providing a visual summary of key supply management data through charts, graphs, and tables. These visuals include inventory levels, purchase orders, and expenditure reports for the selected timeframe.
Activity Diagram – An activity diagram is a graphical representation in the Unified Modeling System (UML) that depicts the flow of activities ir tasks within a system. It provides a visual representation of the dynamic aspects of a system, showing the sequence of activities, actions, decisions and conditions that occur during the processing of records in the system” (UML Specification, Object Management Group (OMG), 2014).
Component Diagram – A component diagram in a system development is a type is a system development is a type of UML diagram that shows the organization and dependencies of the components or modular parts of a system, and how they interact with each other to achieve the system’s functionality.
Context Diagram - These diagrams visually represent the structural relationships between components, showcasing how they interact to achieve specific functionalities or services within the system architecture. The component diagram helps in understanding the high-level structure of the system, emphasizing the interactions and dependencies between components to facilitate system design, development, and maintenance.
Data Flow Diagram - is a graphical representation of the flow of data within a system, illustrating how data moves from one process to another. This visual tool is based on the Unified Modeling Language (UML) Specification by the Object Management Group (OMG) in 2014.
Deployment Diagram - provides a visual representation of how the software components are deployed in a real-world environment, showing the relationships between software and hardware elements. This diagram is valuable for understanding the configuration of the system and how the software components interact with the hardware infrastructure to support the system's functionality.
Flowchart - serves as a valuable tool for illustrating complex processes in a clear and structured manner. It allows for the visualization of sequential steps, decision points, loops, and parallel activities within a system, aiding in the understanding of how tasks are interconnected and how data or materials move through various stages. 
Use Case Diagram - representation of a system's interactions with its users or external systems to achieve a specific goal. It outlines the steps or actions that a user or external system takes to accomplish a particular task within the system. Use cases are typically described in a structured format, including preconditions, main steps, alternative flows, and post-conditions.
HTTPS - Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS) is a system that ensures secure communication over a computer network, particularly the internet. It is designed to provide a secure channel for data exchange between a user's web browser and a website. HTTPS encrypts the data transmitted between the user's browser and the website using Secure Sockets Layer (SSL) or Transport Layer Security (TLS) protocols, preventing unauthorized access or tampering with the information being exchanged.
ISP - is a crucial component of the modern digital landscape, serving as the gateway that connects individuals and organizations to the vast network of the internet. From the author's perspective, an ISP can be viewed as a pivotal system that facilitates access to online resources and enables communication across the global web.
Web Host - A web host can be described as a service that provides the necessary infrastructure and resources for individuals or organizations to make their websites accessible on the internet. From the author's point of view, a web host is like a digital landlord that rents out server space, storage, and bandwidth to store website files and make them available online.

2.3 How the project will work
The proponents had decided to used Agile methodology in developing the project. This will help ensure clear documentation and alignment within the entire project. 
Step 1: Requirements
In this step, the proponent identifies the core functionalities such as inventory tracking, request management, procurement monitoring, clearance processing, and asset disposition. These were discussed with end-users including supply officers, inventory custodians, auditors, and administrative staff to ensure the system would meet real-world needs. Requirements were prioritized based on urgency and institutional needs, allowing the development team to focus first on modules with the greatest impact—such as the automation of supply requests and clearance processes. Regular sprint meetings and feedback sessions ensured the system remained aligned with user expectations, and any changes in requirements were quickly adapted into future iterations.
Step 2: Design
In the Design Phase of the Agile methodology, the focus shifts from gathering requirements to creating a blueprint that will guide the system’s development. Unlike traditional models where design is completed entirely before coding begins, Agile treats design as an iterative process. During this phase, developers, designers, and stakeholders collaborate to create initial wireframes, system architectures, data models, and user interface designs, all of which evolve based on ongoing feedback. The goal is to establish a functional and flexible design that can be adjusted as requirements change throughout the project lifecycle. 
In the context of the Supply and Property Management System, the Design Phase involves mapping out key system components such as the inventory tracking interface, request and approval workflows, asset disposition modules, and procurement tracking features. This includes designing how users like the supply officer, property custodian, and staff will interact with the system.
Step 3: Development 
In this phase, the primary focus is on building and coding the features identified and refined during earlier planning and design stages. Agile promotes an iterative and incremental approach, meaning development occurs in short, manageable sprints where small, functional components of the system are created and tested continuously. During this phase, the development team writes code, integrates modules, and collaborates closely with stakeholders to ensure that what is being developed aligns with user needs and requirements. Regular stand-up meetings, sprint reviews, and feedback loops are essential activities that allow quick identification of issues and immediate adjustments to the development process.
Step 4: Testing
This phase, activities such as unit testing, integration testing, system testing, and user acceptance testing (UAT). Testers work closely with developers to validate each feature as soon as it is developed, ensuring that bugs and issues are identified and addressed early. Agile emphasizes frequent feedback and collaboration, so test cases are updated regularly based on changing requirements and user input. Automation tools are often used alongside manual testing to improve coverage and speed.
Step 5: Deployment
This phase marks the transition from development to real-world use. After thorough development and testing, the system is deployed within the institution to handle actual transactions, such as supply requests, inventory monitoring, asset tracking, and report generation. During this phase, users like the supply officer, property custodian, and auditors are trained on how to use the system, while IT staff ensure that the software functions correctly within the institutional network.
Step 6: Review
In this phase, plays a important role in refining and validating key functionalities such as inventory tracking, procurement processing, request management, and asset disposition. For example, after a sprint focused on automating the supply request module, stakeholders such as supply officers or inventory managers can test the feature, provide feedback on its usability, and suggest enhancements. Their input ensures that the system addresses real-world operational needs, such as reducing transaction time, improving record accuracy, and enabling easier monitoring of supply status.
By following these steps in the Agile methodology, the development team able to incrementally and iteratively build the Supply and Project Management System , incorporating feedback and adapting to evolving requirements throughout the project cycle.

3.4 Architectural Design
Figure 11. Architectural Design
The architectural design of the Supply and Property Management System presented in the diagram follows a multi-tier architecture supported by a TCP/IP network infrastructure. At the user level, a client interacts with the system through a Presentation Layer, which handles the graphical user interface and forwards requests to the Application Layer. This layer is responsible for managing the system’s business logic and communicates with a Web Hosting Server where the actual web application and database are hosted. All internal components, including the Presentation Layer, Application Layer, and Database Server, are connected through a Private Network using TCP/IP, ensuring secure data exchange within the organization.
The system also incorporates an ISP (Internet Service Provider) to connect external users and staff across various workstations (admin, staff, and end-user devices). Admin Workstations are used for managing back-end processes and high-level administrative tasks, while Staff Workstations support routine operations such as supply monitoring, inventory updating, and transaction processing. User Devices, which can be located externally, access the web application via standard TCP/IP connections over the internet.
This architectural setup enables secure and structured communication between the system components while supporting both local (intranet-based) and remote (internet-based) access. It also promotes scalability, centralized data management, and improved system maintenance and security.

CHAPTER IV
Discussion, Conclusion and Recommendation
Discussion
The development of the Supply and Property Management System addresses several inefficiencies commonly associated with manual supply and inventory processes. The system was designed to streamline the management of office supplies by automating tasks such as requisition tracking, inventory monitoring, supply issuance, and report generation. Through a centralized, user-friendly web application, users can easily request supplies, while administrators and supply officers can efficiently update stock records, approve requests, and generate real-time inventory reports.
The system architecture follows a multi-tier design using TCP/IP protocols, ensuring secure and reliable data transmission between client devices, application servers, and the database. The implementation of access-level controls ensures that only authorized personnel can modify critical data, promoting accountability and data integrity. During the testing phase, the system demonstrated improved efficiency in managing requests and reducing delays associated with manual record-keeping.
Although the system achieved its core objectives, it also faced certain limitations. These include restricted access to the system within the institution’s local network and the absence of an automated data migration tool to import existing manual records. Nonetheless, these constraints are reasonable within the scope of the project and can be addressed in future iterations.
Conclusion
The Supply and Property Management System successfully meets the primary goals of the project: to enhance operational efficiency, improve data accuracy, and streamline the overall supply management process within an organization. By replacing manual systems with a computerized platform, the project significantly reduces the risks of human error, loss of records, and inefficiencies in tracking supply usage.
The system also lays the foundation for digital transformation in the supply office by offering a scalable and secure solution that can evolve with the institution’s needs. It enables real-time access to data, supports better decision-making through automated reports, and promotes transparency in the supply chain process.
Recommendations
It is recommended that the system be further developed to include remote access capabilities, enabling authorized users to manage and request supplies outside the institutional network when necessary. Additionally, future iterations of the system should incorporate data import tools to facilitate the migration of legacy records from manual systems, thereby easing the transition for organizations with extensive historical data. Continuous user training and feedback collection should also be implemented to ensure the system evolves based on actual user needs and operational requirements. Lastly, integrating advanced features such as barcode scanning, inventory forecasting, and automated notifications would further enhance the functionality and effectiveness of the Supply and Property Management System.
